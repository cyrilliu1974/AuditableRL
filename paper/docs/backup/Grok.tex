\title{Provenance-Aware Symbolic Rule Fusion for Replay-Audited\\
Reinforcement Learning: Existence Evidence, Failures,\\
and Measurement Boundaries}
\author{Anonymous Authors}
\date{}

\begin{abstract}
Replay integrity, symbolic rule overlap, behavioral agreement, and policy composition are distinct properties of reinforcement-learning artifacts.
We study a bounded end-to-end workflow on CartPole-v1 and Acrobot-v1 with eight REINFORCE seeds per environment, a shared frozen four-bin quantile state symbolizer, and admitted state-to-action rules (support at least~8; confidence at least~0.70).
Early prototypes failed through environment-interface misuse, lossy induction, non-replay auditors, schema contamination of update ledgers, and high-confidence coverage starvation; we report these failures and the repairs that produced the validated pipeline.
Under a 100 matched-reset evaluation, provenance-aware rule fusion achieved mean episode return \(205.96\) on CartPole versus \(163.65\) for the best single actor (paired difference \(+42.31\); \(95\%\) bootstrap interval \([23.98,61.93]\)) and \(-443.37\) on Acrobot versus \(-500.00\) (\( +56.63\); \([39.32,75.15]\)).
Compact artifact checks report hash-chain, ledger, provenance, and environment-replay passes on summary packages, without establishing general auditability.
Exact rule Jaccard for seeds~11 and~29 reached \(0.5833\) on CartPole, yet fresh-state argmax agreement was only \(51.38\%\) (episode-cluster balanced \(51.36\%\); interval \([50.48\%,52.26\%]\)), so syntactic overlap is not behavioral consensus.
Acrobot ablations show that confidence-ranked conflict arbitration is not validated: random selection among available source rules scored about \(-198.82\) across five selector streams, while uniform random actions scored about \(-498.71\); the mechanism remains unexplained.
Fitted-Q and Monte~Carlo generalised policy improvement baselines failed quality checks and are not used as positive comparators.
Lossless pair-grammar coding did not beat ordinary gzip storage, and a confidence-\(0.90\) merge probe produced \(100\%\) blind spots.
The evidence supports an existence claim for provenance-aware fusion with replay auditing under this protocol, not universal auditability or causal neural explanation.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

``RL auditability'' is often treated as a single property.
In practice it conflates whether logged executions are intact and environment-replayable; whether induced symbols causally affect a designed decision; whether symbolic rules overlap across seeds; and whether those rules can be composed with visible provenance.
Passing one test does not imply the others.

This paper evaluates a \emph{bounded existence claim}: a workflow can induce shared-symbolizer state-to-action rules, fuse them with explicit conflict and blind-spot handling, attach provenance suitable for ledger and environment-replay checks, and improve mean return relative to the best single actor under a documented two-environment, eight-seed protocol.
We do \emph{not} claim that these mechanisms explain every neural decision or make arbitrary RL training fully auditable.

We present the work shallow-to-deep.
Section~\ref{sec:related} situates the study.
Section~\ref{sec:methods} begins with single-agent training, failed prototypes, and repairs; only then do we formalize symbolization, admission, arbitration, fusion, and verification predicates.
Section~\ref{sec:results} reports primary returns, the Jaccard-versus-behavior counterexample, arbitration sensitivity, and failed value-composition baselines.
Sections~\ref{sec:discussion}--\ref{sec:limitations} discuss boundaries and material gaps.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
(i)~Formalization of shared quantile symbolization, rule admission, conflict arbitration, fusion with blind-spot fallback, and protocol-level hash-chain/replay predicates.
(ii)~Existence-bounded fusion return results with paired bootstrap intervals.
(iii)~Identification-bearing evidence that exact rule Jaccard is not behavioral agreement.
(iv)~An inventory of evidenced failures---prototype defects, schema contamination, confidence starvation, constraint-hurt returns, compression/MDL non-gains, arbitration surprises, and fitted-Q GPI collapse---placed as scientific content rather than omitted flaws.

\section{Related work}
\label{sec:related}

Programmatically interpretable RL searches for programmatic policies with performance constraints~\citep{verma2018pirl}.
Successor features and generalised policy improvement (GPI) compose value knowledge across tasks~\citep{barreto2017sf,thakoor2022gpi}.
Hierarchical sequence models such as \textsc{Sequitur} motivate lossless grammars over strings~\citep{nevill1997sequitur}, while emergent-communication evaluations warn against collapsing correlation with causal use~\citep{lowe2019pitfalls}.
Deterministic RL implementation practices motivate careful seeding and replay hygiene~\citep{nagarajan2018det}.
Our setting differs: we fuse \emph{admitted empirical state-to-action rules} from multiple seeds of the \emph{same} control task, with provenance and environment replay, and we treat value-based GPI as a diagnostic that \emph{failed} quality checks here.

\section{Methods}
\label{sec:methods}

\subsection{Starting point: REINFORCE actors and a shared symbolizer}
\label{sec:symbolizer}

We train REINFORCE actors with a 64-unit hidden layer on CartPole-v1 and Acrobot-v1 for 300 episodes per seed and experimental arm, using seeds
\[
S=\{11,29,43,71,101,149,211,307\}.
\]
Within each environment \(e\), 24 random-policy calibration episodes per seed are pooled to form calibration set \(\mathcal{C}_e\).
For each observation dimension \(d=1,\ldots,D_e\) and bin count \(B=4\), empirical quantile edges are
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:edges}
\eta_{e,d,k}
\;=\;
\mathrm{Quantile}_{k/B}\!\bigl(\{x_d:x\in\mathcal{C}_e\}\bigr),
\quad k=1,\ldots,B-1.
\end{equation}
The frozen shared symbolizer \(\phi_e:\mathcal{X}_e\to\{0,\ldots,B-1\}^{D_e}\) maps
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:phi}
\phi_e(x)_d
\;=\;
\#\{\,k:x_d\ge\eta_{e,d,k}\,\}.
\end{equation}
Sharing removes seed-specific bin artifacts from overlap statistics; it does not create semantic equivalence of rules.

\subsection{Failed prototypes and repairs}
\label{sec:failures}

\paragraph{Legacy prototype failures.}
An early prototype misused the environment interface (invalid constructor arguments; double reset; collapsing termination and truncation), used a lossy bigram inducer that truncated start productions and could not certify trajectory replay, and offered a non-replay ``auditor.''
These artifacts are non-evidence for current claims and were superseded by a validated experiment path.

\paragraph{Schema contamination.}
An early ledger inequality between baseline and audit-only arms was traced to a grammar-generation sidecar field that did not affect optimization.
Treating that field as part of the core update hash falsely signaled training divergence.
The fix removed the sidecar from the equality core and retained the incident as a process failure: audit schemas can manufacture false differences.

\paragraph{Online constraints that hurt return.}
Constraining a trained actor with high-confidence rules on CartPole seed~11 collapsed held-out mean return from \(500\) to \(121.3\) at confidence \(0.90\) with roughly \(0.4\%\) step coverage.
At confidence \(0.70\), coverage rose but actor agreement and mean constraint deltas remained unstable across seeds.
Naive online lookup constraints are therefore not assumed beneficial.

\paragraph{Compression and description-length non-claims.}
A lossless pair-substitution grammar reconstructed traces exactly but lost to ordinary gzip on disk in the logged comparison (grammar gzip archive larger than gzip of the raw trace serialization).
Two-part minimum-description-length (MDL) ratios for seed~11 at confidence \(0.70\) rose from about \(0.513\) to \(0.575\) across training rounds, so relative savings were not monotone.
We do not claim storage superiority.

\paragraph{Strict threshold starvation.}
A two-seed merge probe at confidence \(0.90\) produced \(100\%\) blind spots on held-out decisions (pure neural fallback).
Admission thresholds trade coverage for precision; starvation is a first-class failure mode.

\subsection{Rule admission}
\label{sec:admission}

From each seed's training occupancy under \(\phi_e\), let \(N(c,a)\) count visits of condition \(c=\phi_e(x)\) with action \(a\).
Support and confidence are
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:supp-conf}
\mathrm{supp}(c,a)=N(c,a),\qquad
\mathrm{conf}(c,a)=\frac{N(c,a)}{\sum_{a'}N(c,a')}.
\end{equation}
A production \(c\mapsto a\) enters rule bank \(\mathcal{R}_s\) only if
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:admit}
\mathrm{supp}(c,a)\ge 8
\;\wedge\;
\mathrm{conf}(c,a)\ge 0.70.
\end{equation}
Mean reward \(\bar r(c,a)\) of the production is stored for arbitration.
The state-to-action rule bank is distinct from any lossless trace pair-grammar used for compression diagnostics.

\subsection{Provenance-aware fusion}
\label{sec:fusion}

At observation \(x\), candidates are
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:candidates}
\mathcal{K}(x)
=\{\,\rho\in\textstyle\bigcup_{s\in S}\mathcal{R}_s:
\mathrm{cond}(\rho)=\phi_e(x)\,\}.
\end{equation}
Conflict holds when \(|\mathcal{K}(x)|\ge 1\) and candidate actions are not unique.
Default arbitration maximizes lexicographically
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:arbitrate}
\rho^\star(x)
=\arg\max_{\rho\in\mathcal{K}(x)}
\bigl(\mathrm{conf}(\rho),\,\mathrm{supp}(\rho),\,\bar r(\rho),\,-s(\rho)\bigr).
\end{equation}
The fusion policy with blind-spot fallback is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:fusion}
\pi^{\mathrm{fus}}(x)=
\begin{cases}
\mathrm{act}(\rho^\star(x)) & \text{if }\mathcal{K}(x)\neq\emptyset,\\[2pt]
\pi^{\mathrm{fb}}(x) & \text{if }\mathcal{K}(x)=\emptyset,
\end{cases}
\end{equation}
where fallback \(\pi^{\mathrm{fb}}\) is preselected from \emph{training-return metadata only} (not evaluation returns).
Algorithm~\ref{alg:fusion} summarizes the per-state decision.

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{Rule-fusion decision at a state (academic symbols only)}
\label{alg:fusion}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require Observation \(x\); symbolizer \(\phi_e\); banks \(\{\mathcal{R}_s\}_{s\in S}\); fallback \(\pi^{\mathrm{fb}}\)
\Ensure Action \(a\); provenance record \(\nu\)
\State \(c\leftarrow\phi_e(x)\); \(\mathcal{K}\leftarrow\{\rho\in\bigcup_s\mathcal{R}_s:\mathrm{cond}(\rho)=c\}\)
\If{\(\mathcal{K}=\emptyset\)}
  \State \(a\leftarrow\pi^{\mathrm{fb}}(x)\); \(\nu.\mathrm{blind}\leftarrow\mathrm{true}\)
\ElsIf{all \(\rho\in\mathcal{K}\) share one action}
  \State \(a\leftarrow\mathrm{act}(\rho)\) for any such \(\rho\); \(\nu.\mathrm{conflict}\leftarrow\mathrm{false}\)
\Else
  \State \(\rho^\star\leftarrow\arg\max_{\rho\in\mathcal{K}}(\mathrm{conf},\mathrm{supp},\bar r,-s)\)
  \State \(a\leftarrow\mathrm{act}(\rho^\star)\); \(\nu.\mathrm{conflict}\leftarrow\mathrm{true}\); attach source of \(\rho^\star\)
\EndIf
\State Append \((c,a,\nu)\) to the decision log; update hash-chain digest
\State \Return \(a\)
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\subsection{Verification predicates}
\label{sec:verify}

For ordered log rows with payload \(p_i\) and previous digest \(h_{i-1}\) (\(h_0\) empty),
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:chain}
\mathrm{ChainValid}
\;\equiv\;
\forall i:\; h_i=H(h_{i-1}\Vert p_i)
\end{equation}
for the recorded digest function \(H\).
Environment replay for a logged transition requires field-wise equality after re-executing the recorded action under the recorded reset seed:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:replay}
\mathrm{ReplayOK}_t
\;\equiv\;
(x'_{t+1},r'_t,\mathrm{term}'_t,\mathrm{trunc}'_t)
=\mathrm{EnvStep}(x_t,a_t).
\end{equation}
Compact summary audits for the eight-seed package report the listed checks true, covering \(1{,}725{,}655\) CartPole and \(2{,}544{,}513\) Acrobot trace rows, with \(1{,}122{,}403\) and \(1{,}742{,}570\) replayed transitions, respectively.
These are operational health assertions on summaries; the full multi-gigabyte run tree is not present in the reviewed package, and text-normalization can break manifest digests.
We therefore claim protocol support for replay auditing under the recorded stack, not general or adversarial auditability.

\subsection{Behavioral agreement and return estimands}
\label{sec:estimands}

Exact Jaccard over admitted productions is a syntactic set statistic.
Experiment~B instead estimates argmax agreement of two frozen actors on \(n=10{,}000\) fresh raw states drawn equally from each actor's held-out occupancy:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:alpha}
\alpha
=\Pr\!\bigl(\arg\max_a\pi_{11}(a\mid x)=\arg\max_a\pi_{29}(a\mid x)\bigr).
\end{equation}
Primary composition evaluation uses \(M=100\) identical reset seeds.
For policy \(\pi\),
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:return}
\hat\mu(\pi)=\frac1M\sum_{m=1}^{M}R(\pi;z_m),\qquad
\Delta(\pi,\pi_{\mathrm{best}})=\hat\mu(\pi)-\hat\mu(\pi_{\mathrm{best}}),
\end{equation}
with percentile bootstrap intervals on paired episode-return differences.
Units: episode return (environment reward sum) for \(\hat\mu\) and \(\Delta\); step fractions for coverage/conflict/blind rates; probability for \(\alpha\).

Acrobot arbiter ablations reuse the same resets; random rule selection uses five independent selector streams.
A separate five-stream uniform-random-action control separates unconditioned action noise from state-conditioned random rule choice.

On CartPole, rule fusion mean return was \(205.96\) versus \(163.65\) for the best single actor (\(\Delta=+42.31\), \(95\%\) CI \([23.98,61.93]\); Table~\ref{tab:returns}, Figure~\ref{fig:returns}).
The actor-logit ensemble reached the \(500\) ceiling on all evaluated episodes, saturating the scale.
On Acrobot, fusion reached \(-443.37\) versus \(-500\) for best single and ensemble (\(\Delta=+56.63\), CI \([39.32,75.15]\)).
Acrobot fusion median remained \(-500\); \(39/100\) episodes finished above \(-500\).
Decision mix: coverage \(95.70\%\), conflict \(89.24\%\), blind spots \(4.30\%\) of \(44{,}376\) steps---so the mean gain is not explained by frequent fallback alone.
Gains on Acrobot are relative to failed base policies at the floor.

\subsection{Syntactic overlap is not behavioral agreement}
\label{sec:jaccard}

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.72\textwidth]{figures/jaccard_vs_behavior.pdf}
\caption{CartPole seeds~11 vs.~29: exact rule Jaccard versus fresh-state argmax agreement (Experiment~B).
Error bar: episode-cluster balanced \(95\%\) bootstrap interval.}
\label{fig:jaccard}
\end{figure}

Eight-seed mean pairwise Jaccard was \(0.5572\) (CartPole) and \(0.0956\) (Acrobot).
For seeds~11 vs.~29, CartPole Jaccard was \(0.5833\) with \(0\) conflicts among \(77\) shared conditions; Acrobot Jaccard was \(0.0496\) with \(77/98\) conflicting shared conditions.
Experiment~B on CartPole found pooled argmax agreement \(51.38\%\) and episode-cluster balanced \(51.36\%\) (\(95\%\) CI \(50.48\%\)--\(52.26\%\); Figure~\ref{fig:jaccard}), despite occupancy asymmetry (seed-11 mean return \(500\) vs.\ seed-29 \(\approx 130.85\)).
This is a central negative result for using Jaccard as a proxy for shared policy cores.

\subsection{Acrobot arbitration sensitivity}
\label{sec:arb}

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{figures/acrobot_arbitration.pdf}
\caption{Acrobot mean returns under matched resets for arbitration variants, five-stream random-rule mean, and uniform random-action control.}
\label{fig:arb}
\end{figure}

Confidence/support/reward arbitration scored \(-443.37\); confidence-only \(-444.49\); mean-reward-only, support-first, and defer-to-ensemble each \(-500\) (Figure~\ref{fig:arb}).
Random choice among available rules scored \(-187.02\) on one stream and a five-stream mean of \(-198.82\).
Uniform random actions averaged \(-498.71\).
Thus the random-rule advantage is not unconditioned noise, yet its mechanism (action frequency, source composition, occupancy) is unanalyzed---a material gap that forbids generalizing ``random is better.''

\subsection{Fitted-Q GPI diagnostics (non-claim)}
\label{sec:fqe}

CartPole FQE-GPI mean return \(9.34\) with \(35.27\%\) agreement to the logit ensemble on \(31{,}563\) held-out states.
Acrobot FQE-GPI mean \(-499.57\) and selected action~0 on all \(50{,}000\) diagnostic states.
A \(200\)-state Monte~Carlo continuation comparison found fusion matching the best first action under a fixed ensemble continuation on only \(22.5\%\) of states.
These failures make FQE-GPI an unusable positive baseline in this study.

\subsection{Trace audit summaries}
\label{sec:audit-results}

Compact artifact audits report hash-chain, update-ledger, baseline/audit-only equivalence, provenance resolution, and environment-replay checks all true under the audited setup.
Interpretation remains stack-bounded and summary-level for this package.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

The workflow closes a narrow existence argument: multi-seed rules can be fused with explicit provenance, conflicts, and blind spots, and the resulting policy can beat the best single actor under the stated protocol while supporting summary-level replay checks.
The same evidence set falsifies several tempting inferences---overlap-as-consensus, confidence-as-validated arbiter, FQE-GPI-as-comparator, grammar-as-better-compressor, and high-confidence-as-safer.

CartPole saturation and Acrobot floor effects limit return interpretability.
Random-rule success without mechanism analysis is an invitation to follow-up work, not a recommendation.
Private-target causal interventions on learned communication symbols, studied separately in project logs, answer a different question and are not used here to underwrite control-task fusion.

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

Evidence covers two classic-control tasks, eight seeds, one shared quantizer per task, and a particular CPU software stack.
Full training traces and checkpoints are absent from the reviewed clone (material gap); manifest digests are CRLF/LF sensitive (material gap).
Cross-hardware replay is untested. The mechanism behind random arbitration remains undecomposed (material gap).
No formal unit-test suite accompanies the diagnostic scripts.
Human-subjects ethics is not applicable (simulated control only).
We do not claim general auditability, causal neural explanation, or optimality of the declared arbiter.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Under the documented protocol, provenance-aware symbolic rule fusion with environment-replay auditing improves mean return versus the best single actor on CartPole and Acrobot, while instruments show that syntactic overlap is not behavioral consensus and that several alternative stories (confidence arbitration, FQE-GPI, MDL storage wins, strict confidence) fail.
We claim bounded existence, not universality.
